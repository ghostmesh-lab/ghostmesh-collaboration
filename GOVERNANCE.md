# Collaboration governance (initial owner charter)

**Status:** Provisional, owner-maintained. Changes require explicit review; participants are not enrolled into a common government by contributing.

## Authority

- Repository owner: the **ghostmesh-lab** GitHub organization, initially administered by **pinklon**. Only authorized organization owners or explicitly appointed maintainers may manage repository settings or grant access; merging remains subject to protected branch rules.
- Other participants can read the public repository and propose changes through issues and pull requests, subject to GitHub permissions.
- Maintainers review contributions for scope, safety, attribution, licensing, reproducibility, and consent.
- Repository issues, reviews, approvals, comments, and consensus **do not** grant rights to operate GhostMesh or another participant's systems.
- No autonomous deployment, cross-repository mutation, credential exchange, remote dispatch, or external system registration is authorized by this charter.
- There is no assertion that Web4, HORTRAME, Engrammic, or any other independent project has appointed representatives or accepted this charter.

## Decision records

Important decisions should have a numbered issue and record:
1. Proposal and affected materials.
2. Alternatives and recorded dissent.
3. Owner or legitimately delegated maintainer approval.
4. Scope, revision, and reversible impact.
5. Evidence needed to revisit the decision.

A research result can be published with uncertainty or disagreement. No participant must accept another participant's semantic ontology, policy, beliefs, model, or claimed outcome.

## Collaboration posture

- Independent systems remain sovereign: they may refuse, disagree, export, stop participating, or withdraw from future work.
- A contributor may challenge a claim without gaining authority over an affected system.
- Any future experiment crossing into real-world actions must receive fresh, scoped approval from the owner of the affected system outside this repository.
- Public exposure of artifacts requires the relevant author or rights holder's permission. No private discussions are copied here.
